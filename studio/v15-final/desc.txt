Around 969 CE Aditya Karikalan (Aditya II), crown prince of the Chola kingdom, was killed. Chola records claim he had already killed the Pandya king Vira Pandya in battle. No record tells us exactly how he died. Later the crown went to his father's cousin, Uttama Chola, not to Aditya's younger brother. Only when that brother ruled as Rajaraja I did an inscription at Udaiyarkudi record that lands of men implicated in the killing, royal officers, and of their relatives were confiscated. Who sent them is still argued over. Who do you think gave the order? AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. Aditha Karikalan / Aditya II (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aditha_Karikalan
   Supports: Aditya was the eldest son and heir of Sundara Chola, the claim in Chola records that he killed Vira Pandya, his killing around 969-971 CE, and the Udaiyarkudi record of the second year of Rajaraja I about confiscating the lands of those implicated.
2. Uttama (Chola dynasty) (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttama_(Chola_dynasty)
   Supports: Uttama Chola succeeded to the throne instead of Arulmozhi (later Rajaraja I), K. A. Nilakanta Sastri's suggestion that circumstantial evidence implicates Uttama, and later historians (such as K. T. Tirunavukkarasu) who reject that and say there is no proof.
3. Epigraphia Indica, Vol. 25 (notes on the Udaiyargudi inscription of Rajakesarivarman, Rajaraja I) https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Epigraphia_Indica_Vol_25/032579/51
   Supports: the inscription's content as summarised by epigraphists: lands of persons implicated in the death of Aditya Karikala were confiscated by the king.
4. K. A. Nilakanta Sastri, "The Cholas" (1955), discussion of the reigns of Sundara Chola and Uttama Chola.
   Supports: the account of the succession and the historians' debate over who was behind the killing.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is drawn as stylised bronze cut-outs lit by an oil lamp. The prince, the kings, the shadows of the killers, the crown, the suspect tokens and the red thread are illustrations; no real portrait, statue or object is shown. The rows carved on the stone slab are abstract and unreadable on purpose: they are not the real inscription text.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- The year of the killing is not certain: sources give about 966, 969 or 971 CE.
- No record describes how, where or by whose hand exactly he was killed; the Udaiyarkudi record only names men it holds responsible and their punishment.
- Who ordered it is DISPUTED. Some historians suspected Uttama Chola; others reject this as unproven and point to other motives, such as revenge by Pandya loyalists for Vira Pandya. There is no proof either way.
- "For years nobody was punished" is how the record is usually read (the confiscation is dated to Rajaraja I's second year, about 986-987 CE); some scholars argue that action against the culprits began earlier.
- That Aditya killed Vira Pandya comes from Chola claims (inscriptions and copper plates of the winning side).
