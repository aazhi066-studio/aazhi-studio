Korkai, near Thoothukudi, was a harbour of the early Pandya kings, famous for the pearls divers brought up from the Gulf of Mannar. Then the Tamirabarani river's silt pushed the land out into the sea. Today the old pearl port is a quiet village about six kilometres from the shore. AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. Korkai (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korkai
   Supports: Korkai in Thoothukudi district near the Tamirabarani; an early Pandya port and pearl fishery; identified with "Colchi" in the Periplus and "Kolkhoi" in Ptolemy; the sea receding about 6 km through silting; Robert Caldwell's 1876 dig finding burial urns; the Tamil Nadu archaeology excavation of 1968-69; charcoal from a trench dated by the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research to about 785 BCE; pottery with Tamil-Brahmi marks dated between the 3rd century BCE and 2nd century CE.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is an illustration made of coloured sand layers. Divers, sailors, the king and the archaeologist are faceless figures; no portrait of Robert Caldwell or anyone real is shown. The marks on the pottery are generic scratches and the attested symbols (fish, bow and arrow, sun), not real Tamil-Brahmi text. Maps and the moving coastline are stylised, not to scale.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- That Korkai was the FIRST Pandya capital is tradition, backed by Sangam references, not by a dated record. The video labels it "tradition".
- The Periplus is usually dated to the 1st century CE; the exact year is unknown.
- The 785 BCE date comes from one charcoal sample from one trench. It dates that layer, not the founding of the port, which is why the video says "one date is not the whole story".
- Sources give the distance from the sea as about 5 to 6 km; the video says "about six".
- When Kayal replaced Korkai is debated (estimates range from about the 3rd to the 12th century CE), so the video gives no date.
