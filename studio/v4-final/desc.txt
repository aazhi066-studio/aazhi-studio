On one night in 1986, a calm lake in Cameroon released a cloud of invisible gas that killed more than 1,700 people. Lake Nyos, the lake that held its breath. AAZHI chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. Benchmarks: August 15, 1984 & August 21, 1986: African killer lakes erupt (EARTH Magazine) https://www.earthmagazine.org/article/benchmarks-august-15-1984-august-21-1986-african-killer-lakes-erupt/
   Supports: the night of 21 August 1986; more than 1,700 deaths; effects up to about 26 km away; carbon dioxide built up in the deep water over decades or centuries; a landslide as a likely trigger; Lake Monoun in 1984; degassing pipes since 2001.
2. Lake Nyos (San Diego State University, How Volcanoes Work) https://volcanoes.sdsu.edu/Nyos.html
   Supports: over 1,700 people plus cattle and wildlife died; CO2 is denser than air and flowed down the valleys; about 23 km affected; the bottom layers overturned; rainwater as a suggested trigger; pipes that siphon deep water.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is an illustration. Villages, people and the lake are stylised; no real victims or survivors are shown. The gas cloud shapes are schematic, and the chart is not to scale.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- What set it off is still debated: a landslide, rainwater sinking down one side, or (a minority view) volcanic gas.
- The reach is given as "more than twenty kilometres" because sources say about 23 and up to 26 km.
- Left out because sources conflict: the cloud's speed, the exact Monoun death toll, and the exact time of night.
