During the famine of 1876 to 1878, starving people in Madras were made to work for their food: about one pound of grain a day. One of the things they dug was a canal through the city. It carries the Governor's name, the Buckingham Canal, not theirs. AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. Buckingham Canal (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Canal
   Supports: the roughly 8 km stretch linking the Adyar and Cooum rivers built in 1877-78 as famine relief work; the canal named after the Governor, the Duke of Buckingham and Chandos, who ordered it; older stretches from 1806 (Cochrane's Canal); total length about 796 km along the Coromandel coast.
2. Great Famine of 1876-1878 (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Famine_of_1876%E2%80%931878
   Supports: the failed monsoon and drought from 1876; death toll estimates across India from about 5.5 million to 9.6 million (official figure about 5.2 million); the relief-work wage from January 1877 of 1 lb of grain plus one anna for a man, less for women and children; Sanitary Commissioner W. R. Cornish arguing for at least 1.5 lb.
3. The Duke who gave us a canal (Sriram V, Madras Musings) https://sriramv.com/2017/10/17/the-duke-who-gave-us-a-canal/
   Supports: the food-for-work programme during the famine that involved digging the canal through Madras, and the canal being named after the Governor.
4. Buckingham Canal: Undoing 150 years of damage (DT Next) https://www.dtnext.in/news/chennai/buckingham-canal-undoing-150-years-of-damage
   Supports: the canal's present neglected, polluted state in Chennai.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is an illustration in the style of a shadow-puppet play. All figures are faceless silhouettes. No portrait of the Duke or of any real person is shown; the Governor is only a hat silhouette and a name plate. Grain, coins, notices and maps are illustrations with no real document text. Maps are stylised, not to scale.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- Death toll estimates vary widely (officially about 5.2 million; later estimates up to about 9.6 million across India). The video gives a range, and no Madras-only figure.
- Sources differ on when the name "Buckingham Canal" came into use (1878 in some, the 1880s in others), so the video only says the canal took his name.
- The 796 km is the whole canal, from Kakinada in Andhra Pradesh. The famine-relief link in Chennai is only about 8 km of it.
- The 1 lb wage was the scale set in January 1877; Madras moved to a revised scale in March 1877.
- No record of the diggers' names is known to us, so the video says "that we know of".
