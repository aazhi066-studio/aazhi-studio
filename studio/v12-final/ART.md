# v12 Serfoji II (Thanjavur Maratha king, 1777-1832) - ART DIRECTION "Tanjore Gilt Panel" (new to the channel)
Story (hedged, matches desc.txt): 1799 treaty handed the government (administration) of Thanjavur to the East India Company; Serfoji kept the fort, title and a pension. Background: adopted as a boy by King Tulaja (Thuljaji); after Tulaja died his half-brother Amar Singh took the throne; missionary Christian Friedrich Schwartz helped protect and educate him; 1798 the British put Serfoji on the throne. Then: greatly expanded the Saraswathi Mahal library (manuscripts + books in many languages); 1805 a press printing Devanagari, OFTEN CALLED the first of its kind in South India; Dhanvantari Mahal medical centre; library holds case notes + 18 drawings of 44 eye patients (Modi script and English), one a cataract operation dated 1827 (Indian J Ophthalmol 2012); WHO operated is NOT CLEAR, some say the king himself (UNPROVEN). Built Manora tower by the sea (c.1814-15) to mark a British victory over Napoleon. Died 1832; his son Shivaji II was the last king; he died without a son (1855) and the British annexed the rest.

Look: the whole film is a cabinet of THANJAVUR-STYLE GILT PANEL PAINTINGS. Every scene sits inside a carved panel: a raised gesso arch frame (cusped/multifoil arch, pillars at the sides) covered in GOLD LEAF, studded with small glass GEM cabochons; inside, flat bold colour fields (no gradients on figures, just flat fills + thin dark brown outline) on a deep ground.

 - Devices NEW to the channel (use several per shot):
   (1) GOLD-LEAF LAYING: thin square gold leaves flutter down (rotating, foreshortening via scaleX=cos) and stick onto shapes; the shape turns gold where they land.
   (2) GESSO BEADING: outlines drawn as rows of raised gold dots that pop in along a path (dot by dot), the Thanjavur 'raised work'.
   (3) GEM INLAY POP: cabochon gems drop into sockets with a tiny bounce and a sparkle star.
   (4) GILT SHEEN SWEEP: a diagonal bright band sweeps across gold areas (clip to gold shapes).
   (5) PANEL CABINET: transitions where panels slide in/out like doors of a temple cabinet, or a panel swings open (scaleX hinge) to reveal the next; arch frames nest (frame-within-frame zoom through the arch).
   (6) VARNISH CRACK / TARNISH: gold dulls to brown and fine crack lines spread = power lost, time passing (used for the 1799 treaty and the 1855 end).
   (7) MANUSCRIPT & TYPE: stacks of palm-leaf/paper manuscripts and bound books on shelves inside arches; a wooden hand press (screw, platen, lever) that stamps; metal type blocks shown as small raised rectangles WITHOUT readable letters (abstract glyph marks only, no invented script text).
   (8) LENS: a round brass magnifier glides over the case notes: inside the lens, an abstract eye diagram (concentric circles: iris, pupil, cloudy lens = cataract shown as milky white disc) and abstract handwriting lines (unreadable dashes) - NO invented record text.
