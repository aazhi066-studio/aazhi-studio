Around 618 the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II marched south toward Kanchi, the Pallava capital, and met the Pallava king Mahendravarman at Pullalur. Who won is disputed, but most accounts agree Kanchi held while northern lands were lost. Around 642 his son Narasimhavarman I struck back: accounts say he defeated Pulakeshin near Kanchi and marched on the Chalukya capital Vatapi (today Badami). Pulakeshin is believed to have died, Vatapi was taken and is said to have been burned, and Narasimhavarman took the title Vatapikonda. AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. Narasimhavarman I (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narasimhavarman_I
   Supports: his reign, the 642 campaign against Vatapi, Pulakeshin II's death in that war, the title Vatapikonda, and the Pallava hold on Vatapi for about twelve years.
2. Battle of Vatapi (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vatapi
   Supports: the 642 attack on the Chalukya capital, the death of Pulakeshin II and the Pallava occupation of the city.
3. Battle of Pullalur (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pullalur
   Supports: the earlier clash between Pulakeshin II and Mahendravarman I near Kanchi, and that the outcome is disputed (Chalukya inscriptions claim a victory, some historians read Pallava records differently).
4. "South Indian kingdoms: Pallavas and Chalukyas", e-PG Pathshala / INFLIBNET https://ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/icp01/chapter/south-indian-kingdoms-pallavas-and-chalukyas/
   Supports: Pulakeshin's attack on Kanchi failing to take the capital, Narasimhavarman's victories near Kanchi in 642 and the title Vatapikonda.
5. Paranjothi (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranjothi
   Supports: that later Tamil tradition names Paranjothi as the general of the Vatapi campaign, identifies him with a saint, and links him to the Vatapi Ganapati image.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is drawn as a carved stone relief. The kings, the general, the soldiers, the cities, the maps and the Ganesha statue are stylised, faceless illustrations; no real portrait, inscription or monument is reproduced. Carved rows standing for inscriptions are abstract and unreadable on purpose.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- The result of the battle of Pullalur is disputed: Chalukya and Pallava records are read in different ways.
- Dates are approximate (Pullalur about 618-619; Narasimhavarman's accession about 630; Vatapi about 642).
- Pulakeshin II's death in the 642 war is widely accepted but is not described in detail by any source; how much of Vatapi was burned is not known.
- Paranjothi's role, his later life as a saint, and the story that he brought home the Vatapi Ganapati statue come from later Tamil tradition, not from records of the time.
- "About twelve years" of Pallava rule over Vatapi comes from some accounts; Chalukya power returned under Vikramaditya I.
