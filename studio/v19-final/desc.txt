In 1801 the Maruthu brothers of Sivaganga, Periya Maruthu and Chinna Maruthu, rose against the East India Company. In June 1801 a proclamation calling people to rise was posted on the walls of Srirangam temple and Trichy fort. By autumn the revolt was crushed, and on 24 October 1801 the brothers were hanged at Tiruppattur fort; accounts say sons and followers died with them. Not everyone was killed. About seventy-three prisoners (some sources say seventy-two) were shipped to Penang, the Company's island off Malaya. Among them, accounts say, was Duraisamy, a young son of Chinna Maruthu (some accounts say of his elder brother). One study says the exiles were held alongside convicts there and struggled for status and dignity. What became of most of them is barely recorded. Mercy, or a punishment worse than the rope? WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is drawn as an inked ledger and indigo watercolour. The brothers, Duraisamy, the soldiers and the exiles are faceless silhouettes; nobody knows exactly what they looked like. The ledger writing and the proclamation sheets are abstract marks, unreadable on purpose: no real document text or names are shown or quoted. The ship, the forts and the sea chart are simplified drawings. The rope and crows are symbols; no execution is shown.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- The number of exiles is given as 73 in some sources and 72 in others; the ship sailed around 1801-1802 (sources differ).
- Whose son Duraisamy was is disputed: some accounts say Chinna Maruthu, others Periya (Vellai) Maruthu. His age is given as 12 or 15. One local history says he was released and returned to India around 1821; this is not well documented.
- Who exactly was executed with the brothers, and how many, varies between accounts.
- What happened to most of the exiles in Penang is not well recorded.
