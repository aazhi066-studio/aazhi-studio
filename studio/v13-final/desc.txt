On the night of 10 July 1806, fifty-one years before the great revolt of 1857, Indian sepoys of the East India Company rose against the British inside Vellore Fort in Tamil Nadu. The trigger was a uniform: new orders in 1805 banned forehead marks, ordered beards shaved and brought a new round hat whose badge was rumoured to be made of forbidden leather. Tipu Sultan's family was held in the same fort. A little after 2 am the sepoys struck; many soldiers of the British 69th Regiment were killed and the flag of Mysore was raised. Within hours Colonel Rollo Gillespie arrived from Arcot, the gate was blown open and the rising was crushed. The dress orders were withdrawn, the Governor of Madras and the army chief were recalled, and Tipu's family was sent to Calcutta. AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

   Supports: the date (10 July 1806) and place; the November 1805 dress regulations (no religious marks on the forehead, beards shaved, new round hat with a cockade rumoured to be of leather); punishment of sepoys who protested; Tipu Sultan's family held in the fort since 1799 and the wedding of one of his daughters the day before; the attack in the early hours; 14 officers and about 115 men of the 69th Regiment killed; the Mysore Sultanate flag raised; Colonel Rollo Gillespie's relief force from Arcot (about 25 km away) riding ahead and the gate blown open by galloper guns; about 350 sepoys killed and about 100 summarily executed; the formal punishments (some blown from guns, others shot by firing squad or hanged); the withdrawal of the dress orders; the recall of Governor William Bentinck and Commander-in-Chief Sir John Cradock; and the removal of Tipu's family to Calcutta.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is a toy paper theatre: the fort, the soldiers, the horsemen, the palace and the ships are stylised, faceless card cut-outs, not real people or places. No real portrait, document or flag is reproduced; the Mysore flag is shown as a simple striped banner. Violence is shown only as toppling card figures, flashes and smoke.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- Casualty numbers differ between sources. About 350 sepoys killed, about 350 wounded and about 100 executed without trial are approximate figures; some accounts give higher totals.
- Accounts differ on the earlier punishment: two sepoys are said to have been flogged (sources give 90 or 900 lashes) and dismissed.
- That the hat badge was made of leather from cows or pigs was a rumour; it is not proven.
- Whether Tipu Sultan's family planned or encouraged the rising is not clear. Their part is debated.
- The exact hour and the number of horsemen with Gillespie vary between accounts.
- Calling it the first sepoy uprising depends on definitions; it is often described as the first large-scale one.
