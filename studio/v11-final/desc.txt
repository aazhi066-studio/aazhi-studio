About 2,000 years ago Muziris (Muciri in Tamil), a port of the Chera kings near the mouth of the Periyar river on the Malabar coast, traded with the Roman world. A Sangam poem pictures Yavana ships arriving with gold and leaving with pepper; Pliny the Elder called it the nearest market of India and warned of pirates; a papyrus in Vienna records a loan for a voyage to Muziris and a cargo worth millions of drachmas. Then the records go quiet. A popular theory blames the great Periyar flood of 1341. Since 2007, digs at Pattanam have found Roman amphora pieces and traces of what may be a wharf, and many scholars think Pattanam may be Muziris. AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. Muziris (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muziris
   Supports: Muziris as a Chera port near the Periyar, its trade with the Roman world, the Sangam poem (Akananuru 149) about Yavana ships bringing gold and taking pepper, the Peutinger map's Temple of Augustus, the decline and the 1341 flood theory, and the Pattanam identification debate.
2. Muziris papyrus (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muziris_papyrus
   Supports: the 2nd-century Greek papyrus in Vienna with a loan contract for a voyage to Muziris and the valuation of the cargo of the ship Hermapollon.
3. F. De Romanis, "Playing Sudoku on the Verso of the 'Muziris Papyrus'" (Journal of Ancient Indian History, 2012) https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/IndOc-Gulf/MuzirisPapyrus-Romanis2012.pdf
   Supports: the cargo value in millions of drachmas, and the argument that pepper made up most of that value.
4. Pliny the Elder, Natural History 6.26 (Bostock-Riley translation) https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/ancient/pliny-india.asp
   Supports: Muziris as the nearest market of India, pirates near Nitrias, ships anchoring far from shore and cargo carried in by boats.
5. Muziris Heritage Project, Pattanam excavations https://muzirisheritage.org/pattanam.php
   Supports: excavations at Pattanam near the old Periyar mouth, Roman amphora sherds (over a thousand), finds read as a possible wharf, and Pattanam proposed as Muziris.
6. Tabula Peutingeriana (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabula_Peutingeriana
   Supports: the map is a medieval copy of a Roman road map and marks a Templum Augusti near Muziris.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is drawn as a tile mosaic. Ships, people (including Pliny), the port, the papyrus, the map and the excavation are stylised, faceless illustrations; no real portrait, document or site plan is reproduced. Rows of dashes standing for the papyrus or the map are abstract and unreadable on purpose. The Sangam poem is paraphrased, not quoted.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- Where Muziris stood is not settled: many scholars think it may be Pattanam, others disagree, and no find has proved it.
- How and when Muziris faded is not known. The 1341 Periyar flood is a popular theory, not a proven cause.
- "Yavana" may mean Greek and Roman traders or western foreigners more generally.
- Cargo values come from damaged papyrus and modern readings differ (roughly 7 to 10 million drachmas); that most of it was pepper is one scholar's argument.
- "About 2,000 years ago" is approximate: the trade peaked in roughly the 1st-2nd centuries CE.
