Rani Mangammal ruled the Madurai Nayak kingdom as regent for her baby grandson for about fifteen years. She paid off a Mughal army, fought Travancore and Thanjavur, and built roads and rest houses that people used for centuries. Her summer palace in Madurai now holds the Gandhi Memorial Museum. But how she died is still a mystery. AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF

   Supports: queen regent of the Madurai Nayak kingdom for her grandson Vijaya Ranga Chokkanatha after her son's death and her daughter-in-law's sati; regency from about 1689; tribute to the Mughals; the 1697 expedition to Travancore and the war with Shahaji, Maratha ruler of Thanjavur; roads (Mangammal Salai), choultries (rest houses), Tamukkam summer palace; her death about 1705 and the folklore that she was imprisoned and starved.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is painted in the style of a kalamkari cloth. Mangammal, her son, her grandson, soldiers and pilgrims are faceless figures; no real portrait of her exists or is shown. The palace, maps, road, rest house and locked room are stylised illustrations, not reconstructions. The 1917 journal page on screen is a drawing, not a copy of the real page.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- Dates differ by source: the regency is given as 1689 to 1704, 1705, 1706 or 1707, and her death as about 1705. "Fifteen years" is approximate.
- The sati of her daughter-in-law and the details of the succession come from later accounts.
- The locked-room starvation story is a later tradition. The 1917 historian who reported it called its source most unreliable. How she actually died is unknown.
- Whether she refused to hand over power, or acted in good faith for the kingdom, is debated.
