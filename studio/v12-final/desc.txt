In 1799 Serfoji II, the Maratha king of Thanjavur, signed a treaty that handed the government of his kingdom to the East India Company; he kept his fort, his title and a pension. Then the king without a kingdom turned to books: he greatly expanded the Saraswathi Mahal library, set up a Devanagari press in 1805, ran a medical centre, the Dhanvantari Mahal, and his library still holds case notes on 44 eye patients, including a cataract operation in 1827. He built the Manora tower by the sea, died in 1832, and after his son died without a male heir in 1855 the British took the rest. AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is drawn in the style of a gilt Thanjavur panel painting. The king, the boy, Amar Singh, Schwartz, soldiers, the press, the library and the tower are stylised, faceless illustrations; no real portrait or document is reproduced. Lines of marks standing for treaties, type or case notes are abstract and unreadable on purpose. The Company seal shown is generic, not the real one.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- Who performed the eye operations is not clear. Some accounts say the king himself operated; the surviving records do not prove it. The technique was possibly couching, not modern lens removal.
- The 1805 press is often called the first Devanagari press in South India; sources describe it differently.
- Sources give different dates for when Amar Singh took the throne (1787 or 1793).
- Some sources tie Manora to the battle of Leipzig (1813), others to Waterloo (1815).
- "He kept his fort, his title and a pension" is a simplification: the treaty left him the fort and a limited area around it.
