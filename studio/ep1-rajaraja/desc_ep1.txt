More than a thousand years ago, a king set out to build a mountain in stone. Episode 1 of AAZHI, the Chola chronicles: Rajaraja Chola I and the Brihadisvara Temple of Thanjavur.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. Brihadisvara Temple (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brihadisvara_Temple
   Supports: built by Rajaraja I between 1003 and 1010 CE, named Rajarajesvaram; granite temple with a 13-tier tower; UNESCO inscription 1987, extended 2004.
2. Rajaraja I (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajaraja_I
   Supports: reign c. 985 to 1014 CE; conquests in the Pandya and Chera countries and northern Sri Lanka; Thanjavur as the Chola capital.
3. UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Great Living Chola Temples https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/250/
   Supports: the complex remains an active place of worship.
4. Wisdomlib, Middle Chola Temples (Rajarajesvara temple, Tanjavur): the copper pot for the finial handed over c. 1010 CE.
5. South Indian Inscriptions vol. II, Tanjavur temple inscriptions nos. 1-2 and 66 (published on whatisindia.com): gifts of gold, ornaments and land; 400 temple women and 108 men assigned to serve the temple.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
All people and scenes are the artist's illustrations. No portrait of Rajaraja survives, so none is shown as real. Marks on walls are decorative, not real inscription text.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- The 80-ton capstone is traditionally said, not proven.
- The temple's height is not stated as fact because sources disagree (about 60 to 66 m).
- The earth-ramp theory of construction is not stated as fact.
