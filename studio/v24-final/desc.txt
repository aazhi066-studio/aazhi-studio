In 1673 the Madurai Nayak ruler Chokkanatha attacked Thanjavur. Accounts say the cause was a refused marriage: Chokkanatha asked for the daughter of the old Thanjavur Nayak king, Vijayaraghava, who had ruled for decades, and was refused. Madurai's army breached the walls. A history of the Nayaks written in 1924, built largely on Jesuit records, says the palace was packed with gunpowder, the royal women were gathered inside so that no enemy could take them, and the palace was blown up while the king rode out with his son and fell fighting. The traveller Niccolao Manucci tells it differently: in his version the attackers burned the palace with the royal family inside. Even the king's death is disputed: killed in battle, or captured and beheaded. Within about three years (1675-76) the Marathas took Thanjavur, ending the rule of both Nayak houses there. AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. R. Sathyanatha Aiyar, History of the Nayaks of Madura (Oxford University Press, 1924), built on Jesuit records; scan on archive.org: https://archive.org/details/historyofthenaya035252mbp
   Supports: Chokkanatha's 1673 campaign against Thanjavur after the marriage proposal was refused; the account that the palace was prepared with gunpowder and the royal women gathered inside before it was blown up; the king's last sally with his son; Madurai installing Alagiri at Thanjavur; the Maratha takeover soon after.
2. Siege of Tanjore (1673) (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Tanjore_(1673) and Vijaya Raghava Nayak (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijaya_Raghava_Nayak
   Supports: the date, the marriage refusal as the stated cause, the gunpowder account (citing Sathyanatha Aiyar), the different versions of the king's death (killed fighting, or captured and beheaded), and the Maratha conquest of Thanjavur in 1675-76.
3. V. Rangachari, "The History of the Naik Kingdom of Madura", The Indian Antiquary vol. 46 (1917) (scan via jainqq.org)
   Supports: Manucci's different version, in which the men of Madurai burnt the palace with the king's family inside, and Rangachari's warning that Manucci is often inaccurate.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
The whole story is painted in the style of a Thanjavur reverse-glass painting. The kings, the princess, the royal women, the soldiers and the palace are symbols, not portraits; no real image of these people or of the 1673 palace is shown. The breaking glass, fire and flashes stand for events the sources describe; nothing graphic is shown. The book pages show abstract lines, not real text.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- Who destroyed the palace is contested: the 1924 history (from Jesuit records) says it was blown up from inside on the king's side; Manucci says the attackers burned it.
- How Vijayaraghava died is disputed: killed fighting, or captured and beheaded.
- The marriage refusal is the cause the accounts give; political rivalry between the two Nayak houses was older and deeper.
- Numbers, exact days and the names of those who carried out the orders vary by source.
