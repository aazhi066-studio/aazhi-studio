In 1772 East India Company troops and the Nawab of Arcot's forces attacked Kalaiyar Kovil and killed the king of Sivaganga. His queen, Velu Nachiyar, escaped with her daughter, lived for about eight years near Dindigul under Hyder Ali's protection, and around 1780 came back with the Maruthu brothers to retake Sivaganga. That part is in the record. The famous story of Kuyili, who is said to have set herself on fire and run into a British gunpowder store, comes mostly from folk songs and oral tradition. 4. "Reclaiming Sivaganga", Open magazine (2017) https://openthemagazine.com/freedom-issue-2017/freedom-issue-2017-dispatches-from-history/reclaiming-sivaganga
   Supports: the 1772 invasion, her flight and the 1780 recovery of Sivaganga.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is drawn in the style of a carved linocut print. Velu Nachiyar, her daughter, Kuyili, Hyder Ali, the Maruthu brothers and the soldiers are faceless silhouettes; no real portrait is shown. The maps, fort, temple, gunpowder store, fire and memorials are stylised illustrations, not reconstructions.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- Kuyili's attack is a story from folk songs and oral tradition. Firm evidence of her life is scarce, and the details (a temple or a fort, ghee or oil, the exact place) change from one telling to the next.
- Dates differ by source: some pages put the king's death in 1780 instead of 1772; the recovery of Sivaganga is given as about 1780; the length of her exile is about eight years.
- Exactly what help Hyder Ali gave (how many soldiers and guns) differs between accounts.
- Popular claims such as "the first suicide attack in history" are not established facts and are not made in this video.
