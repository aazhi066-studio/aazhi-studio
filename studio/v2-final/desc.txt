A king ordered defeated rulers to carry Ganga water to his new capital. What do the sources actually say? Episode 2 of AAZHI, the Chola chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. Gangaikonda Cholapuram (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangaikonda_Cholapuram
   Supports: the capital founded by Rajendra I around 1025 CE, the name's meaning, the temple completed around 1035, the surviving remains (walls, palace site, mounds, broken pillars), and the unclear reasons for the city's decline (Pandya revenge or Delhi Sultanate raids).
2. Brihadisvara Temple, Gangaikonda Cholapuram (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brihadisvara_Temple,_Gangaikonda_Cholapuram
   Supports: Rajendra I as the son of Rajaraja I; the temple dated by inscriptions to about 1035 (some scholars say earlier); the tower slightly lower than Thanjavur's, with the "respect for his father" reading given as an interpretation; the temple still in worship.
3. Middle Chola Temples, Gangaikondacholapuram (WisdomLib) https://www.wisdomlib.org/south-asia/book/middle-chola-temples/d/doc210501.html
   Supports: conquered rulers carrying Ganga water in pots; the water called Chola-gangam, "a pillar of victory in the form of water", in the Tiruvalangadu Plates; the ceremony resting on the king's own praise inscription rather than independent evidence.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
All people, scenes, ships, crowds and buildings are the artist's illustrations. No portrait of Rajendra Chola survives, so none is shown. The map is a stylised route, not to scale. The "inscription" marks are decorative, not real text.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- The Ganga-water ceremony comes from the king's own praise record; no one else recorded it.
- Sources disagree on whether Chola Gangam was a lake or a well, so the video says "water body".
- The temple's exact date (around 1020 to 1035) is debated.
- "Built lower out of respect" is a later interpretation, not proven.
- Why the city fell is unclear.
