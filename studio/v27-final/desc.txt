In 1070 CE Athirajendra had just become the Chola king, and his capital was in turmoil. According to Bilhana, court poet of the Western Chalukya prince Vikramaditya (Athirajendra's brother-in-law), Vikramaditya marched in, put down the unrest and placed him on the throne. Soon after the prince went home, a fresh rising broke out and Athirajendra was killed, after a reign of only a few months. Within days his cousin Rajendra Chalukya of Vengi, whose mother was a Chola princess, took the throne as Kulottunga I, and the old father-to-son Chola line came to an end. Who was behind the killing is still debated. An angry city, or a cousin's plan? AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

   Supports: Athirajendra succeeded his father Virarajendra in 1070, ruled only a few months, faced civil unrest, and was killed in a rebellion; the uncertain later tradition linking the unrest to religion; Kulottunga I came next.
2. Vikramaditya VI (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikramaditya_VI
   Supports: Vikramaditya, married to a daughter of Virarajendra, went via Kanchi, quelled a rebellion and installed his brother-in-law Athirajendra at Gangaikonda Cholapuram; Athirajendra was killed in a civil uprising soon after, making way for Kulottunga I.

   Supports: Kulottunga (born Rajendra Chalukya, an Eastern Chalukya prince of Vengi whose mother was the daughter of Rajendra Chola I) took the Chola throne in 1070, beginning the Chalukya-Chola line.
4. Bilhana (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilhana
   Supports: Bilhana was the court poet of Vikramaditya VI and wrote the Vikramankadevacharita, the literary source for the installation story.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is neon tube line art on a brick wall. The kings, the prince, the crowds, the throne, the crowns, the routes and the scroll are illustrations; no real portrait, building or object is shown. The lines on the scroll are abstract and are not real text.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- The exact length of his reign and the date of his death (usually placed around May-June 1070) are not certain.
- The account of Vikramaditya installing him comes mainly from Bilhana, a poet writing to praise his own patron, so it may favour Vikramaditya.
- Who was behind the killing is DISPUTED. Some link the unrest to religious conflict, based on much later traditions about a Chola king who persecuted a religious teacher; whether that king was Athirajendra is uncertain. Some historians have suspected that Kulottunga, who gained the throne, had a hand in it; there is no proof. No surviving record names who killed him.
- "Within days" follows the usual reading of the dates of Athirajendra's death and Kulottunga's accession, both in 1070.
