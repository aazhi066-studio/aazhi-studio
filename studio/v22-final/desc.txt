Around 1054 (some scholars say 1052), the Chola emperor Rajadhiraja I fought the Western Chalukyas at Koppam, a place usually placed near the Tungabhadra river in today's Karnataka. Chola records say his army was winning when Chalukya archers aimed at the king on his war elephant; he was struck by arrows and killed. His younger brother, Rajendra (Rajendra Chola II), took command, is said to have been crowned on the battlefield, and the Chola accounts say he turned a rout into a great victory. But almost every detailed account comes from the Chola side, and even the date and exact place are debated. Did the Cholas win, or did they pay for it with their emperor? AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. Battle of Koppam (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Koppam
   Supports: the battle between Rajadhiraja I and the Western Chalukyas; the usual date of 1054 and the alternative 1052 (Sen); a location near the Tungabhadra (usually identified with Koppal, Karnataka); Rajadhiraja killed by arrows while on his elephant; Rajendra II taking command and being crowned on the battlefield; the main sources being Chola inscriptions (a 1054 record and Rajendra's Manimangalam record of 1055); the Chalukya account surviving only in a later inscription; the result described as a strategic Chola victory but a tactical Chalukya one.
2. Rajadhiraja I (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajadhiraja_I
   Supports: Rajadhiraja I as Chola emperor, his wars with the Western Chalukyas and his death at Koppam.
3. Rajendra II (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_II
   Supports: Rajendra II as Rajadhiraja's younger brother, his taking command at Koppam, crowning on the battlefield and the Chola claim of victory.
4. Middle Chola Temples, S. R. Balasubrahmanyam (via Wisdom Library) https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/middle-chola-temples/d/doc211905.html
   Supports: the Chola view that Rajendra turned the impending rout at Koppam into a great victory.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is a shadow-puppet play: faceless, stylised leather puppets on rods in front of a lamp-lit cloth screen. They are not portraits of real people. The record tablets are abstract (rows of dots); no real inscription text is shown. Arrows are shown as shadows; no wounds are shown.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- The date: usually 1054, but some historians give 1052.
- The place: Koppam is usually identified with Koppal on the Tungabhadra, but other identifications have been proposed.
- How Rajadhiraja died (arrows while on his elephant) comes from Chola-side accounts.
- The crowning on the battlefield and the "great victory" are claims from Chola records and later Chola praise poetry; the Chalukya side's version survives only in a later record.
- Who really won is debated: some historians call it a Chola strategic win at the cost of their emperor, others a tactical Chalukya success.
