Around 1169 two Pandya princes fought for the throne of Madurai. One, Parakrama Pandya, asked Lanka for help; accounts say he was killed before it arrived. King Parakramabahu I of Lanka sent his general Lankapura across the sea. Lanka's chronicle, the Culavamsa, says Lankapura won battle after battle, put the dead prince's son on the throne and came home a hero. Chola stone inscriptions tell a different ending: that Rajadhiraja II sent an army and the heads of Lankapura and another general were nailed to the gates of Madurai. Each side recorded its own victory. AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. Pandyan Civil War (1169-1177) (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandyan_Civil_War_(1169%E2%80%931177)
   Supports: the war between Parakrama Pandya and Kulasekhara for Madurai, Parakrama Pandya's appeal to Parakramabahu I, his death before help arrived, the Sri Lankan expedition under Lankapura, the Chola intervention under Rajadhiraja II, and that Chola inscriptions say the heads of Lankapura and Jagadvijaya were hung or nailed on the gates of Madurai.
2. Lankapura Dandanatha (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lankapura_Dandanatha
   Supports: Lankapura as Parakramabahu I's general, the Sri Lankan account of his victories and of Vira Pandya placed on the throne, his return home as a hero in Sri Lankan sources, and that Chola inscriptions (such as the Arpakkam inscription) instead say he was defeated and his head nailed to the gates of Madurai.
3. Parakramabahu I (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parakramabahu_I
   Supports: Parakramabahu I as king of Lanka (Polonnaruwa) who sent the expedition to the Pandya country, and the Culavamsa as the chronicle of his reign.
4. Rajadhiraja Chola II (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajadhiraja_Chola_II
   Supports: Rajadhiraja II as the Chola king during the Pandya war and his intervention against the Sri Lankan army.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is a charcoal and pastel drawing. The princes, kings, general and soldiers are faceless, imagined silhouettes, not portraits. The chronicle bundle and the stone slab carry abstract marks on purpose: no real chronicle or inscription text is shown. The gate of Madurai is imagined. No head or body is drawn.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- The two accounts disagree: the Culavamsa presents a Sri Lankan victory, while Chola inscriptions present a Chola victory and Lankapura's death. Both are court records written to praise their own side.
- The date of the head-nailing is given differently (sources mention 1171 and 1174), and the end date of the war differs between sources.
- How exactly Parakrama Pandya died, and in what order the events happened, is reconstructed from these partisan sources.
- Some accounts say the Culavamsa's story of this war breaks off abruptly and does not clearly describe Lankapura's return.
