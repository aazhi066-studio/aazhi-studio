Did the sea really swallow a Tamil city because a king missed one festival? Puhar (Kaveripoompattinam, today Poompuhar), at the mouth of the Kaveri, was the great harbour of the early Cholas, and old Tamil epics describe its ships, warehouses and foreign traders. The epic Manimekalai tells a darker story: a grieving Chola king forgot to hold the festival of Indra, the sea goddess Manimekhala had warned that the sea would take the city if the festival was ever missed, and the poem says the waves took Puhar. That is the legend. The record is quieter: since the 1980s, marine archaeologists and divers have reported stone structures on the sea floor off this coast, and many researchers explain the loss by slow erosion, floods and changes in the sea over centuries. Some have claimed that ruins offshore are more than 11,000 years old; historians are doubtful. AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- The dates of the Manimekalai (often placed around the 6th century CE) and of the Sangam period are debated.
- The flood story is a legend in a poem. Whether a real sea surge, slow erosion or a shift of the river and coast ended old Puhar is not settled, and the city may have declined over a long time.
- The age and nature of the offshore structures are disputed. Claims that they are more than 11,000 years old (made by a popular author in 2002 and by a 2020 study) are not accepted by most historians and archaeologists.
- Which Chola king the poem means, and whether he was a real ruler, is uncertain.
