Around 1231, the Chola emperor Rajaraja III was defeated at Tellaru by Kopperunchinga I, a Kadava chief who had once been a Chola vassal. Records say the emperor, his queen and his ministers were taken prisoner and held at Sendamangalam, the Kadava fortified capital. Rajaraja's ally, the Hoysala king Vira Narasimha II, sent armies into the Kadava country; Kadava towns were sacked, and before Sendamangalam could be besieged, the chief asked for peace. Hoysala inscriptions say the emperor was set free at Tiruvendipuram. Whether he was captured once or twice is still debated, and many historians see the episode as part of the long Chola decline; within about fifty years (1279) the Chola empire was gone. AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is drawn as a kolam, rice-powder line art on a floor. The emperor, queen, ministers, the chief and the soldiers are faceless powder figures, not portraits. The fort stands for Sendamangalam, the coloured powders stand for the sides (yellow Chola, red Kadava, teal Hoysala), and the loop around a dot is a symbol for captivity. No real inscription text is shown.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- The exact year (about 1231-32) and the sequence of events are reconstructed from inscriptions of the Kadava, Chola and Hoysala sides.
- Whether Rajaraja III was imprisoned once or twice has been argued by scholars.
- Some accounts say the Hoysala army defeated Kopperunchinga in battle, others that he sued for peace before the siege of Sendamangalam began; both may describe stages of one campaign.
- Calling it "the start of the end" is a historians' judgement: the Chola decline had several causes, including Pandya and Hoysala power.
