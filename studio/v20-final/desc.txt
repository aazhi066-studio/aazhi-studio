In the 1750s Puli Thevar, the palayakkarar (poligar) of Nerkattumseval in the south of Tamil country, refused to pay tribute to the Nawab of Arcot and the British East India Company. Accounts say the Company's commander Yusuf Khan battered his fort with cannon for weeks, and in 1761 it fell. Puli Thevar slipped away; after Yusuf Khan himself was hanged in 1764, he returned and took the fort back. Around 1767 Company troops under Campbell took it again. Then the story splits. A legend says he was captured, asked to pray one last time at the temple in Sankarankovil, and vanished, leaving only broken chains. School history books say he escaped and died in exile. Some suggest the vanishing story covered up a quiet execution. None of it is proven. AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. Puli Thevar (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puli_Thevar
   Supports: Puli Thevar as palayakkarar of Nerkattumseval and an early opponent of the Nawab and the British; Yusuf Khan's siege once artillery arrived, the fall of his forts (Nerkattumseval, Vasudevanallur, Panayur) in 1761; his return and recapture of Nerkattumseval after Yusuf Khan's execution in 1764; Captain Campbell taking Nerkattumseval in 1767; the legend that he asked to worship at the Sankarankovil temple and disappeared, leaving broken chains; the view that the disappearance is disputed, including the theory that the story was put about to hide an execution.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is an imagined forged-iron illustration. Puli Thevar, Yusuf Khan, Campbell, the guards and the soldiers are faceless silhouettes, not portraits. The tiger stripes on the shield are a design motif (Puli means tiger), not a known emblem. The fort, the temple tower and the map are simplified and not exact likenesses. The book shows abstract marks only, not real text. No body or execution is shown.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- How Puli Thevar's life ended is not known. The temple disappearance is a legend; the textbook says he escaped and died in exile; the cover-up idea is a theory. None is proven.
- Dates differ between sources: his death is given as 1767 by some and later by others, and the length and dates of Yusuf Khan's siege (from about September 1760 to 1761) vary.
- Whether he was captured at all before the Sankarankovil episode is part of the legend, not a firm record.
