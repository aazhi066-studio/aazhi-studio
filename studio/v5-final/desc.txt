A Tamil river dam so old that nobody can say exactly when it was built, and British engineers came to study it. Kallanai, the Grand Anicut on the Kaveri. Episode 5 of AAZHI, the Tamil chronicles.

SOURCES AND PROOF
1. Kallanai Dam (Wikipedia) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kallanai_Dam
   Supports: the dam on the Kaveri credited to the Chola king Karikala; its contested date; built of unhewn (uncut) stone; length about 329 m; repairs and improvements by British engineers in the 1800s (Caldwell, Sim, Arthur Cotton); the name Grand Anicut, from the Tamil "anaikattu" (dam).
2. The Cholas as Hydraulic Engineers (Storytrails) https://storytrails.in/places/the-cholas-as-hydraulic-engineers
   Supports: Karikala's story drawn from Sangam poetry and mixed with legend; the dam dividing the Kaveri to water the delta.
3. Dam standing tall 2000 years (The Better India) https://www.thebetterindia.com/12031/dam-standing-tall-2000-years/
   Supports: Cotton studying the old structure; it being described as one of the oldest water works still in use.

WHAT IS ILLUSTRATION
Everything on screen is an illustration drawn as an engineer's blueprint. No portrait of Karikala exists, so none is shown; the crown emblem is a symbol, not a real object. The figures are silhouettes. The palm-leaf bundle has decorative lines, not real text. Maps and cross-sections are stylised, not to scale.

WHAT IS UNCERTAIN
- The construction date is debated: estimates run from before the common era to about the 2nd century CE.
- Karikala's role comes from Sangam poetry and later tradition, not a dated building record.
- How much of the original stonework survives under the 19th-century repairs is unclear.
- "The oldest dam in the world" is a popular claim; the video says "one of the oldest still in use".
- What exactly Arthur Cotton learned from it comes from popular accounts, so the video says "is said to".
